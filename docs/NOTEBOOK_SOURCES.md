**How they were checked:**
- **Citation checked:** the authors, title, journal, year, and DOI or pages were confirmed against a publisher, PubMed Central or a bibliographic database.
- **Content from:** what the page's claim rests on.
- **Opened and read in part:** four papers were fetched as PDFs and searched for the claims the notebook makes, with their abstracts and the passages around those claims read: Jackson 1978 (opened for the eggs-and-wind page in ROBLOX-80, and again in the audit), and Uma et al. 2013, Shamble et al. 2017 and Girard et al. 2011 (the audit). The rows and sections below say which parts of each.
- **Not read in full:** no paper or book was read cover to cover. Claims were kept to what abstracts, highlights, the passages named, search-result snippets or well-established textbook knowledge state.
- **Textbook, not consulted:** these pages rest on a textbook or review that nobody opened for this notebook, so their wording is the standard account and not a checked quotation. *The bite* (`venom`), *A meal you drink* (`drinking`), *A silk retreat* (`retreat`) and *A new skin* (`new_skin`): Foelix 2011. *Feeling through the feet* (`felt`): Barth 2002 (also the one page the Roblox game cannot open). *A hunter that uses its eyes* (`hunting_by_sight`): Jackson & Pollard 1996, of which only the title and the standard account were seen. ## Ant-mimic page — 2026-10-18 (ROBLOX-64)

**Walking like an ant** (`walking_like_an_ant`, opens when the ant-mimicking spider has stood still with its antennae up for a moment): Shamble, P. S., Hoy, R. R., Cohen, I. & Beatus, T. (2017), "Walking like an ant: a quantitative and experimental approach to understanding locomotor mimicry in the jumping spider *Myrmarachne formicaria*", *Proc. R. Soc. B* 284: 20170308, doi:10.1098/rspb.2017.0308. **Not read in full from the build environment:** the page's claims are the ones the lead (Claude) verified for this round from the paper's abstract and results as supplied: the mimic walks on all eight legs; when stationary its forelegs (legs I) are raised above head height and move in phase like ant antennae (the "antennal illusion"); its stops are short, 83.3 ± 82.6 ms on average, so it seems to move almost constantly (a non-mimic jumping spider such as *Salticus scenicus* is stationary about 55 % of the time, ants about 5 to 10 %); its path is sine-like (wavelength about ten body lengths, range 5 to 10; amplitude about five), statistically like a trail-following ant; in playbacks with *Phidippus audax* as the predator, non-mimic spider targets were attacked 4.5 times as often as ants and 3 times as often as mimics, and ants and mimics did not differ (consistent with protective Batesian mimicry); the playback body length was 5 mm. The page states the stops, the antennae and the playback result; the paths, the stationary shares and the range are in `Data/AntMimic` with their citation. The page says nothing about where the animal lives: no range was verified. What is stylised and said so on the page (`game`): the waving rate and size, the drawn sway of the body (the real path is not walked), the colours and size of the drawing, and that only the female is drawn. Before a public release the paper should be read in full and this page confirmed against it.

**Re-checked in the audit, 2026-10-19.** The authors' copy of the PDF (the Cohen group's page at Cornell) was fetched and searched for each figure above, with the abstract and the results on the stops read; the whole paper was not read. The abstract says the mimic makes "characteristically short (approx. 100 ms) pauses", and the results give "approximately 100 ms (mean stop duration ± standard deviation 83.3 ± 82.6 ms; n = 41)". Also found: the typical wavelength of 5–10 body lengths, the playback ratios (4.5 times as often as ants, p = 0.0055, and three times as often as mimics, p = 0.018), the 5 mm targets, and a non-mimetic salticid stationary about 0.55 of the time. Because the spread is as large as the mean, "about 83 milliseconds on average" read as more exact than the measurement is, and the page's text now says "about a tenth of a second long". The game note and `Data/AntMimic` still say 83.3 ms, because that is the number the drawing's rule is built on (a stop shorter than it is not drawn as a pause), and the game note says so as a rule of the drawing.
