# Feeding follow-up for the next growth increment

Primary paper: Vickers, Robertson, Watson & Wilcoxen (2014), *Scavenging throughout the life cycle of the jumping spider, Phidippus audax*, Journal of Arachnology 42:277–283, DOI 10.1636/J13-31.1. [Publisher PDF](https://www.americanarachnology.org/journal-joa/joa-all-articles/article/download/arac-42-3-277.pdf/?no_cache=1); [author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/272502500_Scavenging_throughout_the_life_cycle_of_the_jumping_spider_Phidippus_audax_Hentz_Araneae_Salticidae).

Table 1 identifies instar 1 as inside the egg sac. Its laboratory feeding protocol offered fruit flies at instars 2–4, then houseflies from instar 5. The offered quantities per feeding were 2, 4, 6 fruit flies, followed by 1, 2 and 3 houseflies at instars 5, 6 and 7–8 respectively. Methods specify three feedings weekly and removal of uneaten prey.

**These are experimental food offers, not meals required to moult, guaranteed consumption, or wild survival odds.** They support a fruit-fly-first implementation. They do not validate the game's 4/6/8/11/15/20 goals, nutrition conversion, compressed clock, starvation deadlines, or failure probabilities. Springtails are an additional game ecology choice requiring their own supporting source.

Implementation inference for the next candidate: offer plentiful reachable fruit flies during the first three playable instars, introduce larger prey later, and define a stage-specific credit unit separately from nutritional reserve energy. Preserve old food records with an explicit currency version rather than silently multiplying saved credit. Test partial feeding across a currency migration, fullness, each prey kind, save/return and new generations before changing the public build. No prey or currency code changed in this research follow-up.
