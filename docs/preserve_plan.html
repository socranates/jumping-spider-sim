The Preserve Plan

The full game's world: a small nature preserve held between a railway, a canal and apartment blocks in the middle of a city. One stud is one centimetre, so this 100 × 60 m preserve is a continent to a one-centimetre spider. Each life is lived in one home range at full detail; corridors join them; spiderlings balloon to new ones. Nothing here is built yet. The current windowsill-and-garden world becomes the test level.

The ten home ranges

Each is a 3 to 5 metre patch built at full spider detail, about the area a real jumping spider lives its life in. Paths, the boardwalk, the wall top, the hedge base and the fence rail are the corridors between them.

Building it, phase by phase

A2 rounds
The skeleton: the preserve's map of zones and corridors in code, the whole preserve drawn at a distance (terrain, trees, the pond, the city, trains as sound), and a switch between the test level and the preserve.
B3–4 rounds
The playable start: the ranger hut and the hut garden at full detail, the nursery rule, the existing life, wasp and rival, and the intro reshot. This becomes the published game.
C3 rounds
The meadow, the path and the bench and lamppost patch; the crab spider; moths at the lamp; ballooning to a new home range as each generation's choice.
D2–3 rounds
The pond edge and the boardwalk; water as a surface; the frog, midges and mayflies; rain.
E3 rounds
The log pile and the veteran oak; the centipede and the ground beetle; bark as a vertical world; the bird.
F2–3 rounds
The stone wall and other jumping spiders as neighbours and rivals; the first extra playable species.
Gongoing
The scrub and the shed, seasons, rare events, more playable species, multiplayer in the shared preserve; beyond the fence for a Steam edition (the canal, the apartment balconies, a rooftop garden).

Questions before Phase A

  1. American or English preserve? The first spider is the North American Phidippus audax, so an American preserve (fence posts, maples, cardinals) keeps it native; an English one has the stone wall, oak, bramble and robin.
  2. Keep the size at 100 × 60 m, or start smaller and denser?
  3. Should every life start in the ranger hut, or only the first, with later lives starting where the spiderlings land?
  4. The cat and the game room were meant for the house. The hut could have the ranger's cat, and a back room for volunteers with a sofa, a games console and board games. Keep them that way?
