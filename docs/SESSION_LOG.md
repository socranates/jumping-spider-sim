### What I fixed

The six failing tests, in the brief's order (the M03 gate and the reeling test share one cause, so there are five causes). Items 1 and 2 were on the test side (a bound and a tolerance below float32's resolution, each measured before it was changed, and a new test added for the second); items 3 and 4 were real bugs and are fixed in the motor, not in the tests; item 5 is an effect of the physics engine, which is now measured, capped and accounted for as that instead of being hidden in a tolerance.
1. **Pendulum energy test, engine error.** GDScript string formatting has no `%g`; the test's own message raised an engine error, which hid the real assertion. The message now uses `String.num`. With the message fixed the check really failed (worst step gain 1.9e-5 against a bound of 1e-6). The bound was below float32's resolution: `Vector3` components are 32-bit, so at a height of 8 to 10 WU the position moves in steps of 2^-20 = 9.5e-7 WU, which is 3.3e-5 of energy (g times that). The bound is now one float32 step of height, `G * 8 * F32_EPS` = 3.3e-5.
2. **"No inward speed is added".** I measured it instead of assuming: the constraint does not push. The leftover along the line is float32 rounding (the test builds the radial direction one way and the constraint re-derives it from target minus pivot), at most 2.3 epsilons of the speed, with no sign and no bias. The tolerance on the radial checks and on "never adds speed" is now 8 epsilons of max(|v|, 1) instead of a fixed 1e-6, which is under float32's resolution at 14 WU/s. A new unit test, `test_the_constraint_residuals_are_float32_rounding_not_a_push`, runs 20000 random taut steps and requires: more than 5000 outward trials, the largest leftover at most 4 epsilons of the speed, a mean within 0.05 epsilons (no bias), and at least 30 percent of residuals on each side of zero. A real push would show as a bias or as something growing with the speed. (The runner prints only the first three failure messages, so the same family also failed on the outward side.)
3. **The tick the body takes hold (M03 F03, F04, F05 and `test_reeling_up_under_the_lip...`).** Two real things, both in the motor. (a) `paid_length` could end above `rest_length` on the grab tick: a reel shortened the rest length earlier in the same tick and `_grab_from_line` returned early. `_grab_from_line` now clamps `paid_length` to `rest_length` and clears `taut`. (b) The trace did not count the reel's work on the grab tick. When the pull carried the body up to a surface it could hold partway through the tick, the trace recorded no reel work and an end-of-line-step energy equal to the start-of-tick energy, though the reel had raised the body. It now records the work of that rise, `g` times the height gained from the start of the tick to the contact, and the existing check that the reel's work stays within what a reel could do (shortening times (g + v^2 / L)) applies to it unchanged. The small step the legs take when they take hold (the body is moved to its seat, at most 0.02 WU, so no visible snap) was already allowed by the test as a signed `hold_work`, and is unchanged. The line does not give the body energy: the reel's work is the rise, and the seat step is the support's.
4. **"Stays at rest".** Real, and in the motor: once a hanging body is still, the motor zeroed its velocity each tick, then gravity added a little before the constraint took it off again, so the body crept down about 7e-5 WU a tick (a time constant of 4.6 s). A hanging body that has been still for 20 ticks (speed under 0.05 WU/s) now rests: it is held exactly where it is and its line keeps being updated, until the player reels or lowers, the line changes (a new bend, a snag), or a surface it is resting against goes away. A first version also woke on any surface change, and the lab's practice perch coming back (a timer) woke it and let it creep again, so it wakes only when a surface leaves and the body is resting touching something. F3 now says "held at rest".
5. **The blocked-reel energy excess (0.0004 to 0.002).** It was not a rounding tolerance, and I did not use one. The excess is the physics engine's own collision recovery: a sphere pressed into a surface is pushed back out by a hair on the ticks it is pressed, which lifts it (13 such ticks in the old fixture, at most 0.000125 WU each; the worst energy excess, 2.33e-3, against the 4.39e-3 that lift is worth). The trace now records that lift per tick (`contact_lift`, capped at 0.002 WU a tick, and a tick that exceeds the cap is a failure), the test's energy allowance includes it, and the M03 report prints how many ticks had it and the largest value per fixture. After the rest fix the old fixture stopped blocking anything: a sphere pushed up against the edge of a plate over half its footprint slides out from under it and takes hold of the slab, which is right. So the fixture was replaced by a coated ceiling with a 0.3 WU square hole, with the line through the hole and the 0.6 WU body unable to follow; the plate case stays as its own test.

### The catch camera check: numbers and verdict

The scenario is M03 F03 in the real game setup (interactive world, real camera rig, jump and line controllers, default settings): standing on the wooden cap of the coated cliff, aim at the practice perch with the controller's aim override, release, the perch drops, the line catches the spider hanging below the cap's lip, then reel (W) either 24 ticks after the catch (a quick player) or once the swing has settled (a patient one) until the grab, and look for 2 s more. NO camera input at any point. Every physics tick `tests/catch_camera_probe.gd` measures the boom length; whether the spider is seen (at least 3 of 5 sample points on its body inside the inner 94 percent of a 16:9 frame and not behind geometry, body drawn); the 1 WU body's height as a share of the screen; whether the lip (where the line bends over the cap's edge) is in frame and unhidden; what share of the line between the lip and the spider is in frame and unhidden; and how much of the screen is covered by geometry within 1 WU of the camera. **A tick is crowded** when the spider is hidden, or the body is taller than 0.6 of the screen (too close), or near geometry covers over a third of the screen, or, while the line is out, the lip is out of frame or hidden, or under half of the line is seen. That is: the player cannot see the spider, the lip it hangs from and the line between them, which is what the choices (reel up, lower, let go) are made from. **Verdict.** At the cliff-cap catch the camera of chunk 5 did hide the recovery choices: the boom, behind the spider and under the overhang, is inside the cliff at every pitch the pitch search tries, so the view is a close-up of rock (0.3 WU boom) with neither the lip nor the line, for the whole time on the line and for 1.5 s after the grab. So I fixed it, but only that. **The fix is a yaw turn, not a pivot or pitch change** (the pitch search is unchanged and cannot help, which is why): while the spider is on a locked line, if the boom at the player's own view is squeezed at the place it will hang (the line's rest point, so the view does not chase the swing) or the lip or the spider cannot be seen, `yaw_assist` turns the view by the smallest turn, in 15 degree steps either way, after which the boom is comfortable (at least 2.5 WU or the camera distance setting, if shorter) and rays from the camera reach the rest point and the lip. At the cliff the smallest working turn is 90 degrees: the view becomes side-on to the cliff face, as in `t06a_after_4a`. It eases over 0.12 s (critically damped, no overshoot), holds a turn while it is still good (no flipping as the rest point moves), is cast like any other boom (a sphere cast, so never through terrain) and never rolls. Any look input (mouse or stick), a recenter or an aim takes the turn over at once and the view stays where it is (it becomes the player's yaw); when the line ends the view stays where it is. ### Perception: what it does

**Layers (spec 19.3, A-010).** World truth (the lab and every `Perceivable` in it) -> `SensorSystem` (evidence) -> `ObservationMemory` (contacts) -> presentation. The sensor reads the world and the spider's eye and gaze, never the camera; it decides no behaviour and writes no words. The memory never looks at the world: a contact changes only when an observation arrives, time passes, or the sensor reports that a remembered spot was inspected and is empty. The presentation reads the memory and the camera; the only thing it asks the world is whether the camera can see a remembered place. The words live in `perception_text.gd` and nowhere else.

**Observation (spec 21.3).** `scripts/perception/observation.gd`: observer, target (only once resolved, i.e. classified at least "possible prey"), estimated position, modality, confidence in [0, 1], the tick it was observed at, category; a record is validated (`problems()`) and an invalid one is refused by the memory. A peripheral record carries no position and no category beyond "movement"; an inspection carries both. A record is never refreshed from hidden truth: a later observation is a new record.

**Peripheral motion.** Anything within 40 WU that moves faster than 0.3 WU/s, lies inside a 300 degree field about the gaze (the 60 degrees directly behind are blind) and has at least one of its five sample points in plain view gives a cue: a direction quantised to a 22.5 degree sector of the spider's own bearing and a 30 degree band of elevation, a low confidence, no position, no category. At most one cue per thing per 0.8 s. The broad scan runs at 10 Hz; anything within 4 WU, and anything already tracked, is checked every tick. A thing behind a wall gives no cue.

**Forward inspection.** A 30 degree cone (15 degrees either side) about the gaze, which is the body's facing. A thing is inspected when its sample points show (five rays: the centre and four points at 0.8 of its radius, a ray stopped by the world more than 0.05 WU short does not count), it is in the cone and it looks at least 0.6 degrees across. Its position is estimated from the points that could be seen. The category refines with continuous exposure (A-004, no species named): "movement" -> "possible prey" after 0.40 s -> "fly-like prey" after 1.00 s, for an easy target (one that looks at least 3 degrees across; smaller ones need proportionally longer). A break shorter than 0.15 s does not reset the exposure; a longer break keeps what was learned in proportion to how much of the memory is left ("something seen a moment ago is not a stranger"). Exposure counts the first inspection tick, so 24 ticks make 0.40 s and 60 make 1.00 s exactly.

**Focus, and the camera does not look.** The cone's axis is the gaze, set by the body. Orbiting the camera never turns it (test: the camera swung 45 degrees for 5 s, the heading changed by 0.000000 degrees). Holding Focus (right mouse, left trigger; the action existed since T01 and did nothing until now) turns the body toward where the camera centre ray points, at most `focus_turn_rate_deg_s` (180 degrees/s: 3.0 degrees a tick), and tilts the gaze up or down by up to 35 degrees (`gaze_pitch_limit_deg`) so a thing above or below can be held in the cone; the tilt comes back level when Focus is released. Walking by the view is as before. `player_controller.gd` (`focus_direction()`, the camera centre ray; `focus_override` and `focus_direction_override` are test hooks), `spider_motor.gd`.

**Memory.** One Contact per track. Live while observations keep coming (an inspection is held for the 0.15 s exposure grace, a cue for the cue cooldown plus 0.2 s), lost after that. A lost contact's confidence falls in a straight line to nothing over 6 s (`memory_decay_s`) and the contact expires then (reason "expired"). A cue never gives a position, never takes back a category and never brings an inspected, lost contact back to life: only another inspection does (the same record, the same track, with `reacquisitions` counted). The **last-seen mark** (A-002) is a lost contact that was inspected: its last observed position, which never moves, because only an observation can move it. It is dropped when the sensor inspects that spot and finds it empty ("empty": the spot inside the cone, all five points in plain view, nothing there, for the 0.40 s an easy target needs); looking away or a spot the world hides does not drop it. Association of an observation with a track is by the percept's id, which is perfect data association (a simplification, question for Chet).

**Presentation (`scripts/presentation/`).**
- `perception_hud.gd`: a pure function (`build_view`) of the memory, the camera and the settings builds the picture, so tests read it without pixels. *Peripheral cue:* a soft glow with a small chevron on the edge of the screen toward the cue as the camera sees it; pulses at each cue and fades over 1.4 s; not drawn for a thing that is already being inspected. *Inspection:* a thin ring at the observed position and its category in plain words under the refinement it has reached ("movement", "possible prey", "fly-like prey"); no numbers, no percentages (the tests check that no word carries a digit or a percent sign). *Loss:* the label becomes "lost sight" at the same place for 2.5 s. *The mark* (setting "Show the last-seen mark", default on): a faint dashed ring at the remembered place, fixed to that world position, fainter and more broken the older it is, gone when the memory is; when the world hides the place from the camera it is drawn at 0.6 of its strength instead of at full (never at the target's true position, never a silhouette). While Focus is held, four small ticks mark the centre of the screen. Nothing at all is drawn for a target that was never sensed, even where the camera sees it (spec 6.4).
- `gaze_overlay.gd` (setting "Show where the spider looks", default off, A-003): a subtle footprint on the first surface along the gaze, within the cone's reach; it stops at occlusion, depends only on the eye, the gaze and the world's geometry, and is identical with the target taken away.

### What changed, by item

1. **Small-target occlusion leak (A-024).** `_clear()` in `scripts/perception/sensor_system.gd` forgave a world hit up to a flat 0.05 WU short of a sample point, so a speck 0.02 WU across with a barrier 0.03 WU in front of it was sensed, all five of its points counted as seen (Chet's probe). The allowance is now `PerceptionMath.sight_tolerance(radius, ray_length) = min(0.05, 0.1 * radius + 1e-5 * ray_length)` WU. **Why these numbers.** The only thing the allowance is for is a sample point lying in the skin of the surface the thing rests on or against (the 0.9 WU test marker touching a wall has points in the wall's face), plus the float error of a long cast. The sample points sit up to 0.8 of the radius off the centre, 0.2 of the radius inside the surface; a tenth of the radius is half of that, so the allowance can never reach a point on the far side of the thing, and a speck's whole body is never inside it. It is proportional because the skin is: the marker keeps 0.045 WU, a speck of radius 0.01 gets 0.001 WU at 1 WU (0.00101). The `1e-5` per WU of ray is the float error of a cast (0.0004 WU at the 40 WU range), so one arrangement gives one answer on every tick. The ceiling `0.05` is chunk 6B's number: nothing is more visible than it was, and the small things lose an allowance they never needed. The unit test states the barrier case as an inequality for every radius from 0.005 to 0.3 WU: the allowance is always less than a barrier 0.02 thick plus the sphere's radius. The spot check (the 0.4 s look at a lost contact's place) shares `_clear()` with the spot's own size (at least 0.2 WU across), so a place behind the same barrier is not called empty. [`tests/integration/test_sensor_occlusion.gd`, 9 tests, on a bare rig (`tests/sensor_rig_case.gd`): Chet's arrangement (eye at the origin, gaze -Z, opaque box centre (0, 0, -0.97) size (0.2, 0.2, 0.02), target centre (0, 0, -1.0) radius 0.01: 0 contacts, 0 visible points, no cue, no inspection); the same speck with the barrier taken away is inspected from five points and refined (possible prey at 1.6 s, fly-like prey at 3.4 s: a speck needs 2.6 times as long as an easy target); a speck of which one sample point is past the barrier's edge is sensed from that point, within the speck, with less confidence; the visible points only fall as a speck slides behind the edge; seven sizes from 0.005 to 0.2 WU radius are all hidden wholly behind a barrier; the marker resting in a wall's face keeps all five points to 0.04 WU inside and none at 0.06; it is never lost and found again by chance; a mark behind the barrier is not called empty and expires at 6 s; and the control, the same mark in plain view, is called empty after 0.4 s]
2. **Who is who (A-027).** Three things were one and are now three: the world's id for a thing (how the sensor finds it each tick, the key of its `_tracks`), the contact (the memory's track id: the spider's hypothesis that some observations are of one visible thing) and familiarity (what exposure to a contact taught, kept per contact and faded with the memory of it; it names no individual). The rule, all in `sensor_system.gd`:
   - *Continuous sight keeps the contact.* A thing's evidence stays under one contact while that contact is live and the thing itself was filed under it within the memory's hold for that kind of evidence: 9 ticks for an inspection (the 0.15 s exposure grace), 60 for a cue (1.0 s). That is the "brief, justified sampling gap". A longer gap is a loss of sight, whatever the world's id says.
   - *After a loss, evidence decides.* The first inspection of a thing that is not unbrokenly linked (it was lost, or is known only from cues) is compared with every lost contact that has a mark. A lost contact could be it when (a) the new place is within `0.5 * (old size + new size) + max(speed bound, the speed the contact was seen moving at) * seconds since it was last observed` of where it was last observed (`PerceptionMath.plausible_radius`); (b) the two sizes are within a factor of 1.5; (c) what was learned is not finer than the thing can ever look (`categories_compatible`: the rank of the contact's category is at most the rank of the thing's `max_category`). It is taken for a lost contact **only when exactly one** could be it; its place and category are then updated by what is seen, and what the earlier exposure taught counts, as much as the memory of it. With none, or two or more, it is a **fresh contact** at "movement", with no exposure carried over; the old marks fade on their own (6 s) or are looked at and found empty.
   - *Never read:* where the thing went, what it did, and its world id after sight was lost. A bare cue has no place to compare, so it is always its own weak contact (`_cue_contact`); the inspection that follows decides, and a contact that was only cues is folded into the lost one it turns out to be (`ObservationMemory.merge`, announced as `contact_ended(..., "merged")`).

### Design choices and tuning values

All of these are data in `data/species/fly_like_prototype.json` unless said otherwise; the few constants left in the code are the three sight rays and their spread (0.8 of the body's radius), the length of the trace (80) and of the event list (64), and the 6 s a vent rests after an entry.

- **Senses:** sight range 14 WU, full sensitivity within 3, front half-angle 90 degrees, rear 150 with gain 0.35; motion threshold 10 deg/s, motion scale 12, loom scale 40; sudden-appearance 18 deg/s within 8 WU; sample gap at most 0.4 s; awareness calm 0.10, alert 0.30, flee 1.00, maximum 1.5; decay 0.35/s relaxed, 0.18/s alert, x3 unseen; threat memory 2 s; vigilance (how much it takes in) 0.5 grooming, 1.0 relaxed, 1.5 alert; startle 70 deg/s within 7 WU.
- **Reaction delays (s), seeded:** already alert 0.04 to 0.10; relaxed **0.16 to 0.36** (first guess 0.10 to 0.22); grooming **0.28 to 0.48** (first guess 0.20 to 0.34); deliberate 0.12 to 0.28. The first numbers were a guess made before any trial; the scripted strike trials below set the final ones so that a good stalk is rewarded most of the time and a leap from far off is not.
- **Flight:** take-off 80 WU/s2 over 0.18 s, flee 15 WU/s, cruise 7, landing 2 (slowing from 1.2 WU), turn 720 deg/s, clearance radius 0.14, lift 0.7, approach 0.8, hop at least 2.5 and at most 26 WU, safe distance 9, keeps 4 WU away from the spider at a perch and 1.5 along a path.
- **Mind:** decision rate 7.5 Hz, minimum commitment 1.2 s, hysteresis 0.15, interrupt at threat 0.5, four candidate perches validated per decision, alert at least 1.0 s before it may relax, restlessness 0.012/s, fatigue +0.08/s in flight and -0.05/s at rest; utility weights rest 0.65 + 0.4 fatigue + 0.1 shelter + 0.15 food - 0.35 threat; move 0.85 restless + 0.3 food + 0.2 shelter - 0.2 travel - 0.45 fatigue - 0.8 threat; flee = threat x (0.9 + 0.25 shelter + 0.3 away - 0.6 travel), worth nothing without a threat.
- **Body and food:** radius 0.3, length 0.7, hover 0.16 WU; capture radius 0.4; nutrition 0.35; feed rate 0.1/s; assist scale 1.6.
- **Population:** delay 25 to 45 s, retry 0.5 s, view margin 12 degrees, vent distance 10 WU, entry flight at least 8 WU.
- **Needs (constants in `needs_model.gd`):** energy start 0.55, drain 0.0006/s, floor 0.20; rest start 1.0, wear 0.0012/s, recovery 0.004/s; update 0.5 s.

**Scripted strike trials** (12 seeds a cell, a still spider, the strike aimed at the fly's perch, final numbers): a calm fly is caught from 2 WU in 11 of 12, from 3 WU in 9 of 12, from 4 WU in 4 of 12 and from 5.5 WU in 0 of 12; a grooming fly in 12 of 12 up to 4 WU and 10 of 12 at 5.5 WU. **After a real stalk** (8 seeds, stalking at 0.8 WU/s, stopping, then striking): stopped at 2.2 WU the fly was unalerted in 8 of 8 and caught in 5 of 8; at 1.8 WU it was alerted in 7 of 8 and caught in 1 of 8; at 1.5 WU it was taking off in 5 of 8 and caught in 0 of 8: **creeping closer than about 2 WU wakes it.** (The two tables differ in set-up: the second spider has been moving in the fly's view for several seconds before it strikes, and stops at a slightly different distance; I did not isolate which matters.) **Through the real controller** (the stick, Shift, the jump button, a stop at 2.6 WU): 8 of the 12 seeds. A walk (2.5 WU/s) head-on alerts a fly at 3.7 to 5.5 WU depending on its facing and reaches 1.0 at about 3.5 WU; a stalk stays under 0.3 until about 2 to 3 WU.

**What the first passes found.** (1) The population's first version let a replacement in where the spider's own eye could sense it (three flies had a contact in their first ticks): it now asks the sensor itself (`could_sense`, the very test a sensed thing passes). (2) Two look-alikes that came into view in the same tick were matched to a lost contact in scene order (7A's unresolved item 2): the matching is joint now. (3) The walk test first expected the fly to take off while the spider was more than 3 WU away and measured 2.7 WU; it now states what was measured (alert beyond 3.5 WU, gone beyond 2.2 WU), and the README says what a player will see. (4) The fly tests wait in game time (a fly is never in a hurry), so what takes about 50 s at `--fixed-fps 60` takes 981 s in real time; the watchdog and the part split had to follow. (5) The first stills used seed 1 at 2.4 WU, where the strike missed: seeds and distances were tried before the scripted hunt was chosen (seed 3), and the captions say it is one run and not an average.
