## What works
What a player sees and does in this build, each line backed by a test or a picture (named in brackets) rather than by the existence of a file.

**Walking and climbing** (chunks 2 and 3): walk on floors, up walls, over convex edges and along undersides; coated (PTFE) surfaces can be stood on but not climbed; an orbit camera that never rolls. [M01: 20 of 20 routes]

**Jumping** (chunk 4, extended here):
- Tap Space: a 1.5 WU hop. Hold Space: a reticle and a landing patch on whatever the screen centre points at; release to leap, Q to cancel. The patch says Reachable (solid green), Blocked (amber, cross), Out of reach (dashed red) or Can't land there (dotted violet), in shape, colour and words, and nothing launches unless the patch said Reachable. [M02: 100 of 100 jumps landed within 0.25 WU of the patch; worst 0.0200 WU. 8 of 8 invalid candidates refused]
- Leaps are much longer than in chunk 4: about 11 WU on the level, 10 WU up onto a 1 WU rise, 9 up 2, 7.5 up 3, about 14 down 3 (the solver's own numbers; one WU is one body length). The pause panel has "Jump reach (playtest)", 50 to 150 percent of that, default 100. The ring grows with distance beyond 6 WU from the camera (up to 4 times) so it can be read across the lab. [`t04_1_long_jump_ring`]
- The lab has two 10 WU towers 9 WU apart and a coated cliff with a wooden cap, to leap between and off. [`t04_1`, `t04_4`]

**The safety line** (this chunk):
- Before the leap, under the patch's label: "Safety line: anchored here" and a white dot on the spider, or, on a surface that cannot hold silk, "No safety line: this surface won't hold silk" and an amber cross. The leap is identical either way. [`t04_2_anchor_ok`, `t04_3_anchor_refused`]
- A leap that lands trails the line behind it and leaves it to fade. A miss (nothing to land on, or something that cannot hold the spider) locks the line and catches the spider: "Caught by your line". It then swings, settles hanging straight below the edge the line passes over, and rests, for good, until the player works the line. [M03 fixtures F01 to F10; `t04_4_hanging_after_a_miss`]
- On the line: W or left stick up reels in, S or left stick down lowers, both at 1.5 WU/s. Reeling up under an edge takes hold of it and the spider climbs on: "Holding on again". Hold Q or the east button for 0.6 s to let go (a tap does nothing). The prompts at the bottom of the screen change with the state. [`t04_5_reeling_up_over_the_bend`, `t04_6_recovered_on_the_ledge`; tests drive the real keyboard and joypad events]
- A player can miss on purpose, recover and try again: the yellow practice perches drop away when you aim at them, and come back four seconds later. [`test_a_player_can_miss_on_purpose_recover_and_try_again`, twice in a row through the real controllers]
- The line never pushes, keeps clear of geometry except where it is snagged (and says so), never adds energy (within the credits listed under Known problems) [corrected 2026-10-07, A-019: the gate is "no unexplained energy gain within the documented allowances", Known problems 5], keeps one bend point, and says why when reeling stops (blocked by something in the way, or the line is caught on a second edge). If the surface it is tied to goes away, it comes loose with a message. [M03: 10 of 10 fixtures; F3 and F4 show the line's numbers and geometry, `t04_4b_hanging_f3_f4`]

**How movement feels, and where it breaks the fantasy.** I have not played this build, and the only judgments of feel on record are Nathan's, about earlier chunks (below). What I can say is from the numbers and the pictures, and none of it is tested by a person. The leap is exact: the spider lands where the patch said (within 0.02 WU in M02's calm, static scenes). That is the point of M02, but it also means a leap has no nerve in it: no wobble, no doubt, and, because the line is free and unlimited, no cost to being wrong. The spider has no body language (no crouch before the leap, no tucked legs in the air, no reaching legs when it takes hold of a lip), so every leap is a sphere following a curve. The catch is silent and soft: nothing jolts, there is no sound, and the view is a close-up of the spider's back at the moment of the catch (tile 5 of `tests/reports/captures/t04_miss_hang_reel_recover_frames.png`, 0.4 s after it) and again just after it takes hold (tile 9); how long that lasts with no camera input I did not measure, because the capture script moves the camera. A settled hanging spider is held perfectly still, which is correct for stopping drift and wrong for something alive. The line is a thin white ribbon that is easy to lose against a pale wall. And reeling is a constant 1.5 WU/s: it is a ladder, not an act. Whether any of this matters is Chet's first question below; the slot after it is for Nathan's own words. ## Known problems
1. **Feel is unjudged by a person** (medium; reproduce: play it). The reel and lower rate (1.5 WU/s: a 3.5 WU reel takes about 2.4 s), the 0.6 s hold to let go, how long the swing takes to settle (2.4 to 5.7 s in the M03 fixtures), the longer leaps, and input lag (a key or button press is applied one to three physics ticks late, up to a frame at 30 fps). All are named constants or data; none can be judged without playing.
2. **The camera at the catch** (medium). Reproduce: `play.bat`, climb the east wall to 5 WU or more, leap west onto the cap of the coated cliff (x 21 to 25, z -9 to -5), turn east, aim at the yellow perch, release. Expected: a view that shows the line and the drop. Actual: the camera is squeezed against the cliff and shows a close-up of the spider at the catch and again at the grab (tiles 5 and 9 of the capture sheet). I did not measure how long it lasts without camera input. Not changed this chunk. [Update, chunk 6A (A-017): measured, then fixed. With no camera input the view of the recovery was crowded for 1.85 s (reel 24 ticks after the catch) or 3.85 s (reel once settled) on the line, and for 1.47 s after the grab; the camera now turns side-on by itself and the figures are 0.53 s and 0.57 s on the line, none after the first 0.83 s, and none in the 2 s after the grab (`tests/reports/catch_camera_report.txt`, `test_catch_camera.gd`). The slab-edge catch (M03 F04) went from 2.35 s to 1.52 s: the swing under the slab and the grab on its underside are still crowded, and are not fixed.] [Update, chunk 6B (A-017, amended): the framing is now capped at 45 degrees, with an off switch in the pause panel. With no camera input the default cliff-cap catch is as crowded as in chunk 5 again (1.83 s and 3.83 s on the line, 1.47 s after the grab), because the smallest turn that clears the cliff is 90 degrees; the slab-edge catch is 2.35 s on the line and 1.53 s after the grab. A view 45 degrees off the open side is helped (0.40 s, none after the grab). `docs/HANDOFF-chunk-6.md`, Known problems 2.]
3. **The line is hard to read in places** (low to medium). It is a thin white ribbon drawn in the world, so in `t04_4_hanging_after_a_miss` it shows only in pieces where the lip hides it (reproduce: any miss, with the default camera). Spec 26 asks for a simple line renderer; whether this is enough is a question for play. [Update, chunk 6A (A-017): the line is now a dark core inside a thin light rim, at least 2 px thick at 1080p (a little heavier while taut or reeling), still hidden by the world where it is in the way, with old silk drawn pale, thin and translucent, and a note in the prompts when the anchor is out of sight behind the lip. Pictures against a pale wall and a dark surface, before and after: `tests/reports/shots/t06a_*` and `t06a_contact_sheet.png`. Not judged by a person.]
4. **A hanging spider at rest is perfectly still** (low). Deliberate: a body still for 20 ticks (speed under 0.05 WU/s) is held exactly where it is, because zeroing a settled body's velocity each tick otherwise let gravity creep it down about 7e-5 WU a tick. It wakes when the player reels or lowers, when the line changes, or when a surface it is resting against goes away. A living spider might sway. (Reproduce: any miss and wait; F3 says "held at rest" once it has settled.)
5. **The "no added energy" check has credits** [corrected 2026-10-07, A-019: call it "no unexplained energy gain within the documented allowances"; the allowances are unchanged] (low, reviewers should know). Per tick the energy may not exceed the start of the tick plus the reel's work, plus the step the legs take when they grab (at most 0.02 WU), plus the physics engine's own collision recovery (it pushes a body pressed into a surface back out by a hair, at most 0.000125 WU a tick in the cases measured; capped at 0.002 WU a tick and measured per fixture in `m03_report.txt`), plus 2e-4. The state is 32-bit, so one step of height at 8 to 16 WU is worth 3.3e-5 of energy. The gate is "no gain beyond these", not "no gain at all". [corrected 2026-10-07, A-019: units: 1 WU = 1 cm = 10 mm, so the cap of 0.002 WU a tick is 0.02 mm and 0.000125 WU is 0.00125 mm. Chunk 5's test comments and the "mm" figures in `m03_report.txt` multiplied WU by 1000, as if a WU were a metre, and were 100 times too large (the cap was written as "2 mm"). Only the words and the report's conversion changed; every tolerance is the same.]
6. **One bend point only** (by design, spec 9.1). A line that would need a second bend snags: reeling stops with a message; lowering and letting go still work (reproduce: M03 F08, or `test_a_line_across_an_obstacle_snags_and_reeling_reports_it_and_never_pulls_through`).
7. **The suite is long** (low). 265 s of real time here; `run_tests.bat` has a 420 s watchdog, so a PC that cannot keep real time could see a timeout failure instead of a result. The text says about five minutes. [Update, chunk 6A: the suite is now 247 tests and about 345 s of real time, run in eight parts; the runner's watchdog was raised to 600 s and `run_tests.bat` says about six minutes.] Every test run above used the default fixture seed 20261005 except the one marked `--seed=777`; there are no saves yet. [Update, chunk 6B: 338 tests and about 560 s of real time, run in ten parts; the watchdog is 900 s and `run_tests.bat` says about ten minutes.]
8. **Practice perches are fixtures** (low). Yellow perches drop when a leap is aimed at them; a real habitat will not do this.
9. **Pre-existing, unchanged:** legs pass into surfaces; no pose for crouch, flight or hanging; a leap from a wall or underside back onto the same plane is Out of reach; hops only start from up-facing surfaces.

## Decisions and deviations
Accepted decisions are in `docs/DECISIONS.md` (D-001 to D-021, A-001 to A-013); chunk 5's proposed entries, which I was told not to write there myself, are listed in `docs/SESSION_LOG.md`, chunk 5, "Proposed ledger entries".
- **Species numbers differ from spec 21.1** (reversible: one data file, `data/species/phidippus_audax_prototype.json`, and what reads it): launch speed up to 20 WU/s (spec 14), longest flight 0.9 s (spec 0.6), line up to 20 WU (spec 8), reel 1.5 WU/s. Why: Nathan asked for farther jumps after playing chunk 4. It amends D-014 (which says 14 WU/s and 21 to 36 ticks). Leaps of 11 body lengths on the level are my tuning by feel and are not checked against the biology in spec 5.
- **Jump reach setting**, 50 to 150 percent, default 100 (reversible: remove the setting). Launch speed limit and longest flight scale by its square root, the line maximum by the setting. For playtests only.
- **The ring scales with distance** beyond 6 WU from the camera, up to 4 times, centre unchanged.
- **Line rules** within spec 9.1: anchor at takeoff on the launch surface at the contact point under the body, only if the surface's profile allows silk and the plan's line fits; no remote aiming; a miss is no contact within 3 ticks of the predicted one or a contact that is not a landing; the line locks at the paid length plus a 0.25 WU run-on; a landing completes the line, which fades over 6 s, at most 6 kept; hold to let go by default (0.6 s) with a 0.4 s grace after the catch, switchable in the pause panel.
- **Biological abstractions and unverified assumptions:** the line is an inextensible, massless constraint that pulls only when taut and can be reeled either way at a constant rate (a jumping spider's dragline is not like this, and nothing here is checked against a source); "cannot hold silk" is a flag on the surface profile, with the PTFE coating as the one lab example (D-010); the practice perches and the 20 WU line are tuning.
- **Test tolerances:** two float32 tolerances were widened with measurements and an added test, and the energy allowance gained one measured credit, the engine's collision recovery (Known problems, item 5; the seat step was already allowed). The reasons are in `docs/SESSION_LOG.md`, chunk 5, "Test changes". No check was removed.

## Next review
Three highest-value questions for Chet:
1. **What should a miss cost?** Today the line is free and unlimited, so a leap into doubt costs nothing and recovery is a ladder. Should P0 charge something for being caught (time hanging, energy, a silk reserve, a moment of exposure) before the P1 silk economy exists, or is a free line the right P0 default because it makes the player brave enough to leap? This decides whether reeling should stay a constant 1.5 WU/s.
2. **Is the farther leap right for the fantasy?** Eleven body lengths on the level makes the lab small; the species numbers now differ from the spec. Keep 20 WU/s as the slice default (and design the habitat for it), return to the spec's 14 WU/s and let the line and the route carry the distance, or leave "Jump reach" as a player setting?
3. **What should the player see and feel at the catch?** The line is the one drawn element in a "no drawn routes" game (A-007). Should it be a clear, hand-readable thing, with the camera pulling back to show the drop and the lip, or should it stay a thin thread that is easy to lose? And is the exact stillness of a settled spider acceptable, or should rest carry a small, cosmetic movement?

Three proposed next implementation tasks:
1. **A feel pass on T04, with Nathan's notes in hand:** the camera at the catch and at the grab, line width and contrast, a reel that eases in and out, the catch's jolt (cosmetic), and a first look at the M04 camera gate on the longer jumps at 30, 60 and 144 fps. Small, and it uses the answers to questions 1 and 3.
2. **T06, evidence-driven perception** (spec 26): observation records, the peripheral cue, inspected classification, visibility checks, debug cones; gate P01. The movement is stable enough to build on, and perception is the next thing the design depends on. [Update, chunk 6B: done; `docs/HANDOFF-chunk-6.md`.]
