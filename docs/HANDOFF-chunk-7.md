**Chunk 7B, the first fly** (`data/species/fly_like_prototype.json` holds every number; no species is claimed).
- *Where.* The played world starts with four flies, on authored perches (21 in the lab; small green boxes in the F4 view): from the spawn, one on the floor straight ahead 9 WU away, one on the open floor further east, one on top of the block, one on top of the second ledge. The console prints "prey seed N"; the same seed gives the same choices and reactions. [`test_prey_fly_rest.gd`, `t07_1_a_fly_on_the_floor`]
- *What it senses.* Only what its own eyes would give it: how fast the spider's body moves across its view and whether it looms (between its own sightings, with its own motion taken out), how near, where in its field of view, and whether three rays from its eye reach the body. Never the buttons, Focus, the camera, the aim, a plan, or where the spider is when it cannot see it. [A01 F1, F2, F5: the pressed jump button, Focus, camera and interact change nothing for the fly; a wall hides a walk and a leap while the same leap in plain view startles it; its scripts name none of the player's things]
- *What it does.* Awareness builds on motion it can see and drains when it cannot: at 0.30 it goes ALERT (a posture you can see: 0.72 s of it before take-off in the A01 run; if nothing more happens it relaxes again after at least 1 s), at 1.00 it flees, and a sudden fast motion within 7 WU startles it at once. Between noticing and taking off sits a reaction delay drawn from its own seeded stream (calm 0.16 to 0.36 s, grooming 0.28 to 0.48 s, already alert 0.04 to 0.10 s). It flies at 15 WU/s to a perch away from the spider, swept against the world each step (never through a wall, never a jump from place to place), lands, and calms down. When left alone it fidgets, grooms for a second or two, and now and then hops to another perch. Its small utility selector (rest, move, flee) has explicit weights, hysteresis, a minimum commitment and an interrupt for credible danger; every change of state carries its winning reason. [`test_prey_fly_behavior.gd`, A01 F3: PERCHED > ALERT > TAKEOFF > FLEE > SEEK_PERCH > PERCHED, each with its reason and evidence]
- *Hunting it.* A walk (2.5 WU/s) alerts it at about 4 to 5 WU and it is gone while the spider is still 2.5 WU or more away (2.7 WU in the test). A stalk (hold Shift, 0.8 WU/s) does not wake it down to about 2 to 3 WU; creeping closer than about 2 WU wakes it anyway. A leap is always seen: the fly startles at once, but it needs its reaction delay to get off the perch, so from 2 to 3 WU the spider often arrives first (8 of 12 scripted trials from 2.6 WU through the real controller; 5.5 WU: never). A grooming fly is the moment to strike. The catch is a swept test, not a dice roll: the spider's body passes within 0.7 WU of the fly (its 0.4 and the spider's 0.3) with nothing solid between, once per strike, one fly held at a time, no matter what the fly is classified as. The pause panel's "Strike assist" (off) widens that reach by 1.6. [`test_prey_hunt.gd`, `test_prey_capture.gd`, the two scripted clips]
- *A miss.* A strike that misses lets the fly escape while the spider recovers: in the test the spider hangs on its line for over a second while the fly takes off, flies 8 WU and lands elsewhere. [`test_prey_hunt.gd`]
- *Eating.* A caught fly is held (CAPTURED); nothing is gained by the catch itself. Standing still on a surface, E (west button) starts a meal: the energy bar (bottom left, subtle) rises at 0.1 a second, 0.35 for a whole fly; moving, jumping, leaving the surface, E again or Q (B) stops it at once and the rest stays in the fly. Energy starts at 55%, drains very slowly to a floor of 20% and nothing starves you. [`test_prey_feeding.gd`, A01 F4, L01 F1 to F4, `t07_5` to `t07_7`]
- *Replacements.* A caught or eaten fly is replaced after 25 to 45 s by one that comes in through a wall vent, only while the vent is outside the picture (plus 12 degrees), outside the spider's sight and 10 WU away, and was so at two looks in a row. A placeholder rule for the later ecology. [`test_prey_population.gd`, 10 tests]
- *Two flies.* Two look-alike flies hidden behind a wall and exchanged (or renamed) while hidden come out as two strangers: the spider's memory is the same whatever the world called them; a lone fly that comes back to the one place only it could be is the one remembered; what one fly takes in and does is the same with or without another fly beside it. [`test_prey_two_flies.gd`, 4 tests]
- *Developer views.* F3 gains Needs, Feeding and Prey lines; F4 draws each fly's label (state, goal, awareness, perch, the winner of its choice, the reason of its last change), its sight line, its flight path in orange, the perches and the four vents. [`t07_8_it_noticed_the_leap_f4`, `t07_9_six_flies_and_the_vents_f4`]
- *Pictures and clips.* `tests/reports/shots/t07_*.png` (nine stills and a contact sheet) and two scripted clips in `tests/reports/captures/`: `t07_hunt_stalk_strike_feed.mp4` (a stalk to 2.6 WU, the strike, the meal; 21.2 s) and `t07_hunt_startled_flees.mp4` (a leap from 5.5 WU that the fly sees coming; 16.6 s), each with a sheet of twelve frames. A script drives the real controllers; the captions and the blue line are the script's. I looked at stills and the frame sheets; I did not watch the videos.

**How it may feel, and where it may break the fantasy (my reading; no person has tried it).** The hunt is a stalk, a short wait with the fly in view, and a strike that mostly works from 2 to 3 WU and sometimes does not, for a reason the player cannot see. The stalk takes about five seconds from 7 WU, long enough that watching the fly (fidgets, grooming) is the game, and short enough that it might feel like a chore; the reaction delays, the alert posture, the grooming rate and the stalk speed were set from scripted trials, not from anyone's hands. A fly is a small dark speck, 0.7 WU long: on a pale floor at 9 WU it may be easy to miss, or too easy to lose again after it flees. The unseen reaction delay can make a good stalk look unlucky. The things most likely to be wrong are all named constants in the fly's data file.

## Known problems
1. **Nobody has played it** (the biggest). Every judgment of feel, pace, fairness and legibility here is a guess: the stalk speed and window, the reaction delays, how long a fly stays alert, the size and colour of a fly, the energy bar.
2. **Chunk 7 has not run on Windows, a real GPU or a physical controller** (medium). The `.bat` launchers have not been run on this build. The suite takes 981 s here and the watchdog is 1500 s, so a slower PC has room; an unknown Windows-only failure is possible.
3. **A strike's result depends on a draw the player cannot see** (medium, a design question). The catch is a swept test, not a roll; what is random is the fly's own reaction delay (0.16 to 0.36 s for a calm fly), drawn from its seeded stream. Reproduce: `play.bat`, note the console's "prey seed N", hold Shift and creep to about 2.6 WU of the floor fly, hold Space with the ring on its perch, release. Expected: a strike that usually works. Actual: about two in three; when it fails the fly saw the leap and was going. Scripted: 8 of 12 seeds through the real controller at 2.6 WU; from a still spider at 2, 3, 4 and 5.5 WU, 11, 9, 4 and 0 of 12; after a real stalk to 2.2, 1.8 and 1.5 WU, 5, 1 and 0 of 8 (creeping closer than about 2 WU wakes it).
4. **A "lost sight" ring follows a catch** (low). The memory is not told the fly was caught, so the ring behind the spider's head says "lost sight" for up to 6 s (`t07_5_caught_and_held`). Reproduce: catch a fly. Expected: no ring for a fly that is in the jaws.
5. **Flies the spider cannot tell apart stay strangers** (low, by design; A-027). After a loss, a fly that could be either of two remembered places is neither, and both old marks linger up to 6 s. The F4 view shows ids the player is never told.
6. **One speed bound for every contact** (low; item 6 of chunk 7A, not done). The region a lost thing could have reached grows at the larger of 2.0 WU/s and the speed it was seen at; a fly first seen perched that takes off while hidden comes back as a stranger.
7. **The replacement rule is a placeholder** (low): four wall vents, 25 to 45 s, never in view. It is not an ecology.
8. **The suite is long, and three runs are close to the call limit** (medium, for whoever maintains it): the gates run takes 138 s and integration parts 10 and 12 take 139 s and 137 s against 175 s. Split to more parts with the next large addition.
9. **The fly's model is cosmetic** (low): a dark body, red eyes and a pale wing; wings up for ALERT. No species is claimed and nothing about it is known to read well on a real display.
10. **Scratch files:** the VM cannot delete files, so `tests/reports/shots/_to_delete/` holds old stray pictures (the ones of this chunk are overwritten with 1 pixel images) and `data/prey/` is an empty folder. Safe to delete.
11. Chunk 7A's notes that a toggled Focus ends with a hop and that `max_category` is read as how a thing can look, and the items of chunks 6B, 6A and 5, stand (`docs/SESSION_LOG.md`, `docs/HANDOFF-chunk-6.md`, `docs/HANDOFF.md`): feel unjudged, the line's readability, a perfectly still hanging spider, the 45 degree catch framing, practice perches as fixtures, legs through surfaces. 7A's item 2 (two sightings of one tick for one lost contact) is fixed in this chunk; its item 4 (a fly out of sight must be hidden, not despawned) is met: a living fly always gives a percept and a captured or eaten one gives none, which is also why the "lost sight" ring of item 4 above appears.

## Decisions and deviations
Nothing was written to `docs/DECISIONS.md` or `docs/SPEC.md`. The ledger entries I propose are in `docs/SESSION_LOG.md`, Chunk 7B, "Proposed ledger entries".
- **One kind of prey, "fly-like prey", with designed numbers.** Sight range 14 WU, a startle at 70 degrees a second within 7 WU, awareness thresholds 0.10, 0.30 and 1.00, flight at 15 WU/s and the rest are prototype values in `data/species/fly_like_prototype.json`, not measurements of any insect (`biological_status`: `prototype_behavior_no_species_claim`). Reversible by editing the file.
- **The reaction delays were lengthened after scripted strike trials** (calm 0.10 to 0.22 s became 0.16 to 0.36 s; grooming 0.20 to 0.34 s became 0.28 to 0.48 s) so that a good stalk has a fair chance and the trial results are above; a guess about what feels fair, not a finding.
- **The catch is deterministic geometry,** one event with a stable id per strike (`capture:spider:<fly>`), with a strike assist that is off by default and a classification that never gates a strike (as the brief asked).
- **Feeding is gradual and cancelable;** the catch grants nothing; a meal only runs while the held prey is CAPTURED, the spider is attached and still; cancel (Q, B) belongs to the meal while it runs. Energy: start 55%, drain 0.0006 a second to a floor of 20%, 0.35 per fly: nothing in this build needs a meal. Rest exists in the needs model with no consequence yet.
- **Replacement flies come in only by four wall vents** and only unseen (P0 rule above); the shipped world has four flies, test worlds none unless a test asks, and the magenta test target is separate.
- **The sensor's matching of sightings is now joint** (a lost contact is taken only when it is one sighting's single candidate and no other sighting of the tick could be it): a consequence of two flies, in the spirit of A-027.
- **The runner's watchdog is 1500 s** (was 900) and the gate group takes `test_a0*.gd` and `test_l0*.gd`.
- **Biological abstractions:** awareness as one accumulator, vigilance as a multiplier by posture, the utility weights, the perch preferences (shelter, food blots) are design values. A cue, a prompt or a label that names "fly-like prey" is the spider's reading, not the fly's.

## Next review
Three highest-value questions for Chet:
1. **Is watching and approaching a living fly enjoyable?** The loop is: notice a speck at the edge of the view, Focus on it, creep up for about five seconds while it fidgets and grooms, strike, eat. Is there enough in the fly to watch, or is the stalk a wait? What should change first: the stalk speed, how often it grooms, how soon it calms after a scare, what the player can see of it?
2. **Does its escape feel fair?** It reacts only to what its eyes took in, and a leap is always seen, so a strike from 2 to 3 WU catches it about two times in three and a strike from far off never does. Is that the right spread? Should the player get a tell before a fly goes (the reaction delay is invisible), or is not knowing part of the hunt? And is the strike assist (a reach of 1.12 WU instead of 0.7) an accessibility option or a crutch?
3. **Can the player explain a hunt's result?** F4 tells a developer exactly why every fly did what it did. Without F4 the player sees a posture, a take-off, a flight and "Holding prey". What should the game show, in its own language, so that a player can say why a fly fled or was caught (E02)?

Three proposed next implementation tasks:
1. **T08, the windowsill loop:** the authored habitat in place of the lab arrangement, two routes, a retreat (which gives the unused rest reserve its first use), onboarding prompts, ambient cues and one nonlethal disturbance. The 21 lab perches become the sill's; the four placeholder vents become its ways in. Likely first.
2. **A feel and fairness pass on the fly, with Nathan's notes in hand:** constants only (reaction delays, stalk window, alert time, relaxation, reach), plus two small fixes this chunk found: tell the memory a fly was caught (no "lost sight" ring) and take the contact speed bound per kind of prey from its data. Small; it could go first if Nathan finds the fly unfair.
3. **T09, persistence and accessibility:** validated save and restore (which also answers the half of L01 that is not run, and S01 and S02), a non-visual form of the edge cue and of the fly's alert, text size and reduced motion, remapping.
