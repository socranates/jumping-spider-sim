# The garden's bestiary — more bugs, more kinds, a page for each (proposed round W3b)

Nathan, 2026-10-19: "More more more bugs and types of bugs and information on them." This is the list to build from, after W3 (garden
life: the fungus gnat, the aphid and the woodlouse, which set the pattern: a prey profile derived from the fly's golden, or an ambient
bug with its own small module; a body under the fly's part budget; a generated notebook page with named sources). Each entry says what
the bug is in the game, its size at the game's scale (1 stud = 1 cm; the first spider is about 1 cm, the peacock about 0.5), where it
lives, what it does, and what its page can honestly say with a source the builder must open. **Built so far** (marked *built* in the tables):
the springtail, the leafhopper, the small moth and the crane fly, part A of W3b (ROBLOX-101, 2026-10-19: `docs/DECISIONS.md` has the numbers,
`docs/NOTEBOOK_SOURCES.md` the pages' sources); and the ladybird, the millipede, the garden snail and the harvestman, part B (ROBLOX-105, 2026-10-19:
ambient bugs, not food, each a seeded sim of its own with a body and a page; a strike at one, or at a woodlouse, is refused in a short note). The rest is not built. The honesty
rule stands: a sentence without a source is left out, and the page says "In this game" where the game simplifies. The bodies' motion was audited
and the biggest gaps filled in ROBLOX-128 (the table near the end).

The jumping spider's own rules decide the roles: it hunts by sight what moves and is smaller than itself, it avoids ants and armoured or
chemically defended things, and it is itself hunted by wasps, larger spiders, centipedes and birds (sources: Foelix, *Biology of
Spiders*, 3rd ed.; Jackson & Harland on salticid prey choice; Nelson & Jackson on ant aversion — the builder opens the editions and
cites pages).

## How it is built (the W3 pattern, repeated)

- Prey: a derived profile per kind (from `fly_like_prototype`, the Godot golden untouched), a body in `FlyBodies`/`FlyLook` under the
  fly's part cap, `Catch.factorFor` and a meal size, a seeded population per region with its own perch kinds (soil, leaf top, leaf
  underside, stem, flower, wall, glass, air). Walkers (aphid, caterpillar, thrips) use W3's perch-to-perch walk; jumpers (springtail,
  leafhopper) get an escape hop on the fly's reaction model; fliers use `FlyFlight`.
- Ambient: `Shared/Prey/Bugs/*.luau` with one small sim each on W3's woodlouse pattern (seeded, regional, hides and returns, uncatchable
  or left alone for the sourced reason), bodies in `Client/BugBodies`.
- Threats: each is its own decision and round (the wasp took two rounds); the centipede at the stone, the orb web and the crab spider are
  the first three, after W4's rules exist.
- Pages: every bug has one, through `tools/gen_notebook_pages.py`'s ROBLOX_PAGES, sources in `docs/NOTEBOOK_SOURCES.md`, triggered by
  the first good look (the sensor's first identification at close range, or the Inspect card), so the notebook fills as the garden is
  explored. The page says what the bug is, what it does, and what a jumping spider does about it; "In this game" says where the game
  simplifies (a sixth of a meal is a game number, not a measurement).
- Budget: per-region caps on living bugs at full and low tier (the phone is the limit); far regions keep none.
- Scale: the kinds that outsize a 1 cm spider (crane fly, millipede, snail, bee, centipede) are the ones that make the garden feel big
  from the spider's eye, which is the point; their bodies cost parts, so each is budgeted alone.
