- *Peripheral cue.* Anything moving in a 300 degree field about where the spider looks gives a soft marker on the edge of the screen toward it and nothing else: no place, no name; at most one cue per thing per 0.8 s; nothing from behind a wall. [`t06_2_peripheral_cue`, P01 F1]
- *The camera is not the gaze.* Swinging the camera toward the cue changes nothing: the heading and the cone did not move over 5 s. [P01 F2]
- *Focus* (right mouse, left trigger): the body turns toward where the camera points, at 180 degrees a second at most, and the gaze tilts up to 35 degrees. In the 30 degree cone the thing is classified by how long it has been in view: "movement", "possible prey" after 0.40 s, "fly-like prey" after 1.00 s (an easy target; a small one takes longer). A thin ring and plain words, no numbers. [`t06_3`, `t06_4`, `t06_5`, P01 F3]
- *Memory and the last-seen mark.* Out of sight it says "lost sight", and a faint dashed ring stays at the place it was last inspected: it never moves, gets fainter and more broken, and is gone after 6 s; hidden behind the screen, the target walks on and the mark does not follow it. Looking at the empty place in plain view drops the mark (0.40 s); looking away does not. When the target is seen again it is the same record, known again faster. [`t06_6` to `t06_9c`, P01 F4 to F7]
- *Nothing for what was never sensed,* even where the camera sees it. [P01 F8]
- Settings (pause panel): "Show the last-seen mark" (on), "Show where the spider looks" (off: a footprint where the gaze lands, `t06_10_gaze_footprint`), "Frame the line after a catch" (on). **How it may feel, and where it may break the fantasy (my reading; no person has tried it).** The design says a jumping spider has to turn to look, so the player's camera is free and the spider's look needs a held button. That makes a second action to learn: swing the camera at the cue, see nothing change, then hold Focus. The classification pace (about half a second to "possible prey", one second to "fly-like prey") and the marker's speed (0.8 WU/s, a guess) were chosen to leave time to notice, turn and watch; they could be too slow, or the edge marker could be too easy to miss on a pale wall. The cues are visual only. All of these are named constants in the species profile (`data/species/phidippus_audax_prototype.json`) or in `perception_hud.gd`.

## Known problems
1. **Nobody has played it** (the biggest). Every judgment of feel, pace and legibility here is a guess: Focus, the 0.40 s and 1.00 s classification, the marker's speed, the edge marker and label legibility, the mark's strength.
2. **The 45 degree cap leaves the default cliff-cap catch crowded** (medium). Reproduce: `play.bat`, climb the east wall to 5 WU or more, leap west onto the cap of the coated cliff, turn east, aim at the yellow perch, release, do not touch the camera. Expected: a view that shows the line and the drop. Actual: as in chunk 5, the camera squeezed against the cliff, 1.83 s (quick reel) or 3.83 s (reel after settling) on the line and 1.47 s after the grab, because the smallest turn that clears the cliff is 90 degrees. The slab-edge catch is the same (2.35 s, 1.53 s after the grab). A view already 45 degrees off the open side is helped.
3. **Track association is by the thing's id** (low now, high for T07). A hidden thing that returns is always the same record. Fine for one target; the prey brain needs a rule for when "the same thing" is in doubt. *(Update, 2026-10-09: no longer by id. Chunk 7A (A-027) keeps the world id, the contact id and the category's familiarity apart: after a loss, a returning thing is matched to a lost contact by evidence, and only when exactly one fits; otherwise it is a fresh contact. `docs/SESSION_LOG.md`, Chunk 7A.)*
4. **Focus aims by the camera's centre ray,** which stops at the first thing it meets: one cannot aim through the screen at what is behind it. Not explored further.
5. **Cues have no non-visual form** (medium for accessibility). The sound-off baseline holds only because there is no sound yet.
6. **Perception cost is unmeasured:** rays per tick are counted (`rays_this_tick`, `max_rays_in_a_tick`) for one target; no budget.
7. **Scratch files** in `tests/reports/shots/_to_delete/` (the VM could not delete them): safe to delete.
8. Chunk 6A's and chunk 5's items stand (`docs/HANDOFF.md`, `docs/SESSION_LOG.md`): feel unjudged, the line's readability, a perfectly still hanging spider, one bend point, the suite's length (now about ten minutes), practice perches as fixtures, legs through surfaces.

## Decisions and deviations
Nothing was written to `docs/DECISIONS.md` or `docs/SPEC.md`. The ledger entries I propose are in `docs/SESSION_LOG.md`, Chunk 6B, "Proposed ledger entries".
- **Focus also tilts the gaze** by up to 35 degrees (not asked for): it lets a target above or below the ledge be held in the cone; it returns level.
- **The test target's default speed is 0.8 WU/s,** a guess at what a person can follow (1.4 WU/s left no time to notice, turn and watch).
- **A mark whose place the camera cannot see is drawn at 0.6 of its strength, not hidden:** hiding it removed it in the main scenario (a target behind the screen).
- **The edge marker is dropped for a thing already being inspected;** the ring takes over.
- **Perfect data association** (by id) is a simplification, not a design (Known problems 3). *(Update, 2026-10-09: replaced in chunk 7A.)*
- **The F4 view draws where the target truly is,** for the developer only; it is the one place truth is drawn.
- **The catch framing is capped at 45 degrees** (A-017) with an off switch: measured, it does not help the default cliff-cap catch.
- **The runner's watchdog is 900 s** (was 600; the suite is about 560 s here) and the gate group also takes `test_p0*.gd`.
- **Species numbers for perception are designed values,** prototype hypotheses in the species profile, not measurements of any spider (`fly_like_inspection_s` 1.0, `inspect_cone_degrees` 30, `peripheral_cone_degrees` 300, `memory_decay_s` 6, `focus_turn_rate_deg_s` 180, and so on; the list is in the SESSION_LOG).
